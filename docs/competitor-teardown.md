# Competitor teardown: Sage, Xero, Zoho

What the three sites do well, the psychology behind it, and how each point is applied to the ManaGEM site. Live rendering of the three sites was blocked by the build environment's network policy, so this draws on their indexed pages and documented structure. Points marked "verify" should be checked against the live sites before they are quoted anywhere.

## The patterns they share

| Pattern | What it looks like | Why it works | Applied to ManaGEM |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One job, said plainly | Xero: "The all-in-one to get your business done". Sage product pages lead with the plan and the trial. | Reduces cognitive load. A visitor decides in seconds whether the page is for them. | Hero keeps the brand line "Know what's making you money" and a one-sentence job description. |
| Risk reversal next to every ask | Xero: 30-day free trial, no credit card, cancel any time, 24/7 support. Sage: 30 days free, no card, month to month, no long-term contracts, commitments or hidden fees. | Removes the three fears behind a trial click: money, lock-in, abandonment. | Hero assurance row (14 days free, no card, cancel anytime, set up in an afternoon). "14 days free, cancel anytime" under every plan button. "Month to month, no lock-in" promise. |
| Segment the visitor early | Sage footers and nav split sole trader, small business, medium business, accountants and bookkeepers. Xero runs a separate accountants and bookkeepers track with a partner programme. | Self-identification. People trust a product that names their situation, and the site can speak in their vocabulary. | New "For your trade" section with five trade tabs, each with outcomes, the modules they will use most and a product crop. A dedicated accountants and bookkeepers card routes to the team. |
| Anchor the pricing | A highlighted middle plan, prices per month with VAT treatment stated, promotional framing (Xero: 80% off for three months, verify current offer), add-ons priced per user (Sage). | Anchoring and the centre-stage effect push most buyers to the plan the vendor prefers. Stating VAT and billing period avoids surprise, which kills trust. | Team card featured as "Most popular", every price marked excl. VAT, a yearly toggle with "2 months free", an expandable comparison table and an add-ons list with placeholders for field users and extra companies. |
| Make the comparison available, not forced | Xero and Zoho put a detailed plan comparison behind a "compare plans" step rather than on the first screen. | Progressive disclosure. Scanners get cards, evaluators get the table. | "Compare plans in detail" disclosure beneath the cards. |
| A written trust pledge | Zoho: no ads, never sells data, own data centres, GDPR pages per product. Sage: refund within 30 days, Support and Training column, community. | Trust is built from specific, checkable promises rather than adjectives. | "Our promises" band: data stays yours and is exportable, POPIA aligned with daily backups, month to month, people who build it answer. |
| Support as a feature | Sage lists Support, Contact, Training and Community in every footer. Xero puts 24/7 support in the trial terms. | Buyers of business software are buying the relationship after the sale. | Support appears in the header, in the promises band, in the FAQ ("How do I get help once I'm in?") and as a footer column. |
| Many ways to say yes | Xero and Zoho close with trial, demo and contact side by side. | Different buyers are at different stages. One button loses the undecided. | Final section offers three paths: start a trial, talk to the team, see how it works. |
| Persistent call to action on phones | All three keep the primary action reachable while scrolling on small screens. | Mobile visitors rarely scroll back to the top. | Sticky bottom bar with "Start a free trial" and "Pricing", hidden while the form or footer is on screen. |
| Footers as site maps | Columns by product, audience, support, company and legal, plus region and currency. | Signals a real company and gives late-stage readers the legal and contact pages they look for. | Five-column footer with legal placeholders and a region line: South Africa, English, ZAR excl. VAT. |

## Psychology notes applied in copy

- **Specificity beats superlatives.** "14 minutes from first upload", "42 hours over", "R 232k over 60 days". Numbers are remembered; adjectives are not.
- **Loss framing before gain framing.** The "Why it matters" section leads with what a business loses without visibility, then the features show the gain.
- **Consistency of the ask.** Every primary button says "Start a free trial". Changing the label at each step makes people re-decide.
- **Social proof in the buyer's own words.** Customer stories are written as outcomes, by role and trade, with the module they live in. They remain drafts until approved quotes exist.
- **Reciprocity.** The AI take-on is positioned as work done for the visitor before they pay.

## What was deliberately not copied

- Promotional discounting (Xero's percentage-off framing). ManaGEM's commercial terms are not frozen, and discount framing anchors the perceived value downward for a product that is already the cheapest line item in a contractor's month.
- Large subscriber counts. ManaGEM cannot claim them, and a small true number beats a vague large one.
- Mega menus. The page is a single scroll; a mega menu would add weight without routes to go to.
